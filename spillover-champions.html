Spillover champions

Which of the world's top R&D investors generate the most valuable knowledge spillovers per innovation — globally, to high-income countries, or to emerging and developing economies? Pick the outcome, how many firms to show and the minimum portfolio size. Hover a bar for the firm's details; the chart's camera icon saves a PNG.

What is shown

Firms
Companies in the EU Industrial R&D Scoreboard (the world's largest R&D investors) matched to their patent families in PATSTAT; one bar per firm, averaged over its innovations (patent families, patents of invention only, filed 2013–2022) with a Watson valuation. The diamonds (optional, top axis) show the selected spillover as a share of the firm's global spillovers, i.e. the fraction of the knowledge flows its innovations generate that is received in the region selected; the dashed line marks the mean of that share across all firms passing the filters, not only the top ones shown. Bar width can be set proportional to the firm's number of innovations in the window, or to its logarithm, so that firms whose averages rest on large portfolios stand out; very thin bars are widened slightly so that their labels stay legible.
Spillovers
The expected value of the knowledge flows an innovation generates for later innovations, in USD million per innovation, following the Patent Rank approach of Martin, Verhoeven et al.: "global" counts recipients anywhere, the regional variants only recipients located in the region named. EMDE excludes China throughout; the "excl. China & India" variant additionally removes spillovers received in India.
Private value
The expected private (stock-market) value of the innovation to its owner, USD million per innovation.
Caveats
Averages over few innovations are noisy, which is why the minimum portfolio size defaults to 30. Firm names are the Scoreboard's; subsidiaries are folded into the parent where the Scoreboard lists them together.
